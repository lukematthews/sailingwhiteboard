SailingWhiteboard

Strategy & Positioning (v1)

⸻

1. The Problem

When sailors debrief, they physically move objects around on tables to tell the story.
	•	“You were here.”
	•	“We came in on port.”
	•	“Overlap established here.”
	•	“Then contact.”

It’s intuitive. It’s visual. It works.

But:
	•	It’s not persistent.
	•	It’s not shareable.
	•	It’s not replayable.
	•	It’s not structured.
	•	It doesn’t scale beyond the room.

There is no purpose-built, simple tool designed specifically for telling sailing stories clearly.

Most sailing software is:
	•	Data-heavy (GPS analysis tools)
	•	Race management-focused
	•	Or overly complex animation software not built for sailing logic

There’s a gap.

⸻

2. The Core Intent

Make telling stories about what happened on the water easy.

Not “build an animation editor.”
Not “build sailing software.”

Build the digital version of:

Moving coasters around a table — but better.

⸻

3. Primary Audiences

1️⃣ Sailing Schools

Use case:
	•	Teach right-of-way
	•	Explain mark roundings
	•	Demonstrate starting strategy
	•	Show common mistakes

Why it matters:
	•	Visual learning sticks.
	•	Instructors can build a repeatable lesson library.
	•	Students can replay scenarios later.

⸻

2️⃣ Coaches

Use case:
	•	Post-race debrief
	•	Show tactical decisions
	•	Compare “what happened” vs “what should have happened”

Why it matters:
	•	Faster clarity.
	•	Cleaner communication.
	•	Shareable recap with athletes.

⸻

3️⃣ Protest Rooms (Strong wedge)

Use case:
	•	Reconstruct incidents.
	•	Show overlap establishment.
	•	Clarify sequence of events.
	•	Present a structured timeline.

Why it matters:
	•	Protest committees need clarity.
	•	Written descriptions are messy.
	•	Hand drawings are crude.
	•	This could become the default visualization tool.

This may be the strongest initial positioning wedge.

⸻

4. Value Proposition

SailingWhiteboard makes it easy to:
	•	Create clear, visual reconstructions of sailing situations.
	•	Define key moments in time.
	•	Replay events.
	•	Share stories beyond the room.

It combines:
	•	Simplicity of table-top storytelling
	•	Structure of timeline animation
	•	Sailing-specific logic and terminology

⸻

5. Product Wedge

The wedge is not “animation.”

It’s:

Structured sailing storytelling.

Specifically:
	•	Steps as story beats.
	•	Ghosted boats at each key moment.
	•	Tracks showing flow between moments.
	•	Clear numbering on canvas.
	•	Scrubbable time.

The dope sheet exists to support the story — not dominate it.

The canvas is the table.

⸻

6. Product Principles

These should guide every design decision:

1️⃣ Story First

If it doesn’t make storytelling clearer, it doesn’t belong.

2️⃣ Fewer Controls, Clearer Moments

Users care about:
	•	Key moments.
	•	What changed.
	•	Who was where.

Not dense keyframe editing.

3️⃣ Immediate Visual Clarity

At a glance, someone should understand:
	•	Boat positions.
	•	Sequence.
	•	Direction of movement.
	•	Who was inside/outside.

4️⃣ Fast to Create

It must be:
	•	Faster than drawing on paper.
	•	Faster than explaining verbally.

5️⃣ Shareable

A story that can’t leave the room loses half its value.

⸻

7. Long-Term Vision

If executed well, SailingWhiteboard becomes:
	•	The default protest visualization tool.
	•	The default debrief tool for sailing schools.
	•	A coaching staple.
	•	A shared language for race storytelling.

Eventually:
	•	Templates for common scenarios.
	•	Scenario library.
	•	Export to PDF/video.
	•	Embedded sharing.

But all of that rests on one thing:

Make telling a sailing story effortless.

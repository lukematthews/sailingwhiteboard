1. Pitch Version (Clubs / Coaches / Schools)

SailingWhiteboard — Tell the Story of the Race

Sailing debriefs have always worked the same way:

People gather around a table and move whatever’s nearby — coasters, forks, markers — to explain what happened.

It’s simple.
It’s visual.
And it works.

But it’s also:
	•	impossible to share
	•	hard to replay
	•	not structured
	•	gone the moment the conversation ends

SailingWhiteboard is the digital version of that table.

A lightweight tool designed specifically for sailors to:
	•	reconstruct incidents
	•	explain tactics
	•	teach rules
	•	run clearer protest hearings
	•	share race stories instantly

Key Features
	•	Step-based storytelling (not complex animation)
	•	Ghost boats at key moments
	•	Tracks showing movement between steps
	•	Scrubbable replay timeline
	•	Simple export + sharing

Who It’s For
	•	Sailing Schools — better lessons, reusable scenarios
	•	Coaches — clearer debriefs, athlete improvement
	•	Protest Rooms — structured reconstructions, faster clarity

The Goal

Not “video editing.”

Just:

Make telling stories about what happened on the water easy.

2. Focused MVP Roadmap (Aligned to Strategy)

This is the MVP if the goal is tabletop storytelling → shareable replay.

⸻

Phase 1 — The Table (Core Storytelling)

Must feel like moving coasters around.

Canvas Basics
	•	Drag boats
	•	Rotate boats
	•	Marks + start line
	•	Wind arrow

Steps (Story Beats)
	•	Add step
	•	Select step
	•	Ghost boats at every step
	•	Step numbers visible on canvas

Playback
	•	Scrubber
	•	Play/pause
	•	Animation starts at step 1 (not time=0)

Output:
A coach can recreate an incident in under 2 minutes.

⸻

Phase 2 — The Story (Clarity + Sharing)

Visual Clarity Upgrades
	•	Tracks between steps
	•	Highlight selected step
	•	Snap + alignment polish

Sharing
	•	Export “Story Link”
	•	Export PDF of steps
	•	Export short replay video/GIF

Output:
A sailor can send the story to someone not in the room.

⸻

Phase 3 — The Protest Wedge

Protest Mode
	•	Step = “Moment of Incident”
	•	Labels: “Overlap established”, “Contact”, etc.
	•	Rule references
	•	Printable protest diagram pack

Output:
Committees start asking for it by name.

⸻

Phase 4 — Scenario Library + Templates
	•	Common starting situations
	•	Mark rounding drills
	•	Protests as templates
	•	Coaching playbooks

3. Pressure Test: Should Protest Rooms Be the Wedge?

Honestly?

Yes — probably the strongest wedge.

Here’s why:

⸻

Coaching + Schools = Broad, Slow Adoption
	•	Lots of casual users
	•	Many ways to “kind of” do this already
	•	Harder to monetize quickly
	•	Value is real but not urgent

⸻

Protest Rooms = Sharp Pain + No Alternative

Protest hearings are:
	•	stressful
	•	ambiguous
	•	time-sensitive
	•	dependent on clarity

And right now the tools are:
	•	paper sketches
	•	verbal chaos
	•	low-quality diagrams

If SailingWhiteboard becomes:

“The standard way to show an incident”

That’s a very strong foothold.

⸻

Protest Rooms Create Authority

If protest committees adopt it, then:
	•	sailing schools follow
	•	coaches follow
	•	clubs follow

It’s a top-down legitimacy wedge.

⸻

Protest Feature Set is Small but Powerful

You don’t need a huge product.

You need:
	•	steps
	•	ghost boats
	•	clear timeline
	•	printable/exportable diagram

That’s it.
